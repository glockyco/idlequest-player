# Boss kills

How the coordinator decides which bosses and named enemies the account kills, how, and when. A boss kill is one more way to get gear and quest pieces, valued in the currency of [gear acquisition](gear-acquisition.md), hours of a group's progress, so it compares with hunting, camps and visits.

## Problem

- The daily list's two free summons went unused every day until 2026-11-10, and the raids since were started by hand. A casino potion waits for a hand too, and the bot had no way to use one.
- The bosses also spawn in the open world, and nothing hunts them there.

## What is known, and how

Verified in the game or in the client's code:

- A group of six kills some bosses without raid mode: group 1 at levels 51 and 52 killed the open-world Lady Vox at 20:52 on 2026-11-10 in normal play, and Tagujazolu gained 88,539 XP (G1's game log).
- Raid kills pay the leader XP: Phinigel Autropos 390,451 per kill, Lady Vox 435,600 (G1's game log).
- A daily summon makes a private copy. The client's panel: "Two free resets per account every rolling 24 hours, with one kill per boss. Resuming a boss or retrying a wipe is free. Casino credits and existing potions do not use free resets. Private bosses last 24 hours; nearby enemies remain shared."
- An open-world kill sets the boss's daily cooldown too: after the 20:52 kill the daily list showed Lady Vox on cooldown until 20:52 the next day.
- Raid recruits fought while their own groups kept playing them (the raids of 2026-11-10).
- Raid time is bought by the hour, quoted at 8,000 pp with the highest character at 52 (`iq.raid.time()`).
- A casino potion is used with the client's consume function (`ds` in `Bip1bGL8.js`), which sends `casinoPotion: true`. The client's casino table names each boss's zone and spawn point.
- A casino credit is spent with the Raid Resets window's credit request in its boss's zone, which also resumes the account's casino boss free while it stands (`iq.raid.bosses(key, { credit: true })`, docs/plans/systems-research.md, "Boss summons and daily allowance").

Open-world spawns, from the server's content tables: respawn times of about 12 h for Phinigel Autropos and Lodizal, 72 h for Lady Vox, Lord Nagafen, Venril Sathir, Trakanon, Innoruuk, Cazic-Thule and Lord Yelinak, 168 h for Vulak`Aerr, spawn chance 0 for the four Warders, and no open-world spawn for Tunare and the Avatar of War. Each respawn is drawn inside the spawn point's variance around that time (`variance` in `spawn2`: 1.2 h for Phinigel Autropos, 7.2 to 21.6 h for the 72-hour bosses). The raids of 2026-11-12 killed Lady Vox, Innoruuk and Trakanon 60.4, 66.3 and 78.6 hours before the respawn times the server then gave. The spawn list names the respawn time of a boss that is down (`nextRespawnAtMs`), so it is exact once a tab has seen the boss down, and a kill is dated from it only to twice the variance. The server can put bosses up ahead of these times: after its restart at 21:15 on 2026-11-12, six of the seven bosses the tabs saw stood one to two days early, and Venril Sathir kept his time. Seen live in the tabs' spawn lists on 2026-11-12: Phinigel Autropos alive at his point in Kedge Keep, Innoruuk respawning in the Plane of Hate. The server gives a zone's spawn list only to a character in that zone (a request for another zone answers "Your zone is out of sync. Resynchronize before loading enemies."), so a boss's state is known from the tabs that are in its zone.

A raid target stands apart from AutoLevel: the client marks it `autoLevelExcluded` in the spawn list, and it still attacks a party that fights inside its aggro radius. Phinigel Autropos joined 51 fights of the groups in Kedge Keep in a day, all of them pulls of the four swirlspine seahorses that spawn 111 to 163 units from him (aggro radius 200), and won all 52 fights with him since midnight of 2026-11-12. The bot keeps AutoLevel off the spawn points inside such an enemy's aggro radius (`guardedSpawns` in `src/bot.js`).

## Design

### Sources and ways

A boss kill has a source and a way. Sources: the open-world spawn on its respawn timer (shared with other players), a private copy from a free daily summon (two a day) or from a casino potion (its holder's). Ways: the boss's zone visited by one group in normal play, or a raid of all 72 characters in raid mode, which needs raid time. Each pair is a candidate, and the coordinator takes the cheapest way that kills.

### Kill chance

The raid simulator fights each candidate with the surveyed characters: the group's six for a group kill, all 72 for a raid. A background job refreshes the results after each gear survey and keeps them in the db meta key `raidOdds`, so a decision never waits for a fight. A candidate counts as killable when every simulated fight kills it. The first live kill of each kind checks the simulator, as group 1's Lady Vox did for a group of six. A live wipe holds the candidate until the next survey changes the roster, and the fight's trace names the cause.

### Value and cost

A kill is worth the XP it pays and the items of its loot table at their drop chances: each copy the account's demand still needs (`docs/plans/items.md`) and each gear upgrade at its `copyValue`. A group kill costs the group's trip and fight time against its hunting. A raid costs the leading group's time and its share of the raid hour, which covers every raid kill in it.

### Schedule

- Open-world spawns: a boss of the daily list, or one whose drops the gear plan wants, that a tab sees alive at its spawn point is killed there by a raid, without a summon, so its summon stays for another boss (`openBosses` in `tools/lib/boss-plan.mjs`, the db meta key `bossesSeen`). Each snapshot of a tab carries the raid targets of its zone's spawn list. The raid looks at the zone's list before it buys anything and ends when the boss no longer stands. A raid costs the fleet little: the groups' XP per minute fell by 0.4 and 0.6 group-hours over the 25 minutes around the raids of 2026-11-11, inside its usual swing.
- The watch: twelve of these bosses have a live spawn point (`watchedBosses`, from the content's spawn tables), ten of the daily list and Lord Yelinak and Vulak`Aerr, who are on no list, so the open world is the only place the account kills those two. Each is one kill more than the two free summons a day, worth 0.1 to 2.9 hours of a group's progress on 2026-11-13, and 21 for Lord Yelinak. Other players kill them too: of the seven the tabs had seen by 2026-11-12, six were down, and Phinigel Autropos, who came back at 10:39 on 2026-11-13, was dead between 13:55 and 15:02. So a group looks (`nextLooks`, the bot's `look` task): it travels to the boss's zone, reads the spawn list and goes home, which took 12 to 78 s in the looks of 2026-11-12. A look goes to every watched boss the raid kills in the simulator, whether or not an hour of raid time can be bought then, because without it the plan knows neither that a boss is worth an hour nor how long it stands. First comes a boss whose respawn is due, ahead of it and staying past it (`LOOK`), and one whose sighting is older than the server's last reset of the spawns (`spawnReset`: a boss seen alive ahead of its respawn time voids the older sightings of bosses that were down, as does a restart of the server, which the collector notes when every tab was out of the world and one is back, `worldOutage`). Bosses due together go by what their kill is worth, and a look from before a reset holds none back. Then comes a boss that stood more than half an hour ago and the plan would raid now, then one no tab has seen, then one that stands and the plan would not raid, every two hours. A pass sends up to four looks, each with a group of its own, and up to ten in the half hour after a reset, when many bosses stand at once and other players are on their way. In that half hour the next pass follows at once while a boss waits for a look or a raid. A look that finds its boss standing brings the coordinator's next pass at once, and the strongest group travels there to lead the raid, its hunt stopped for it. The sightings are kept (`bossLog`), and the dashboard's "Bosses in the open world" shows each boss, whether its respawn has passed without a look, when it comes back, and between which sightings someone killed it. Open: how long a boss stands, of which Phinigel Autropos's three to four and a half hours are the only measurement, the danger of a zone's entry to the group that looks, whether the leader reaches a boss deep in its zone, and whether the server lets a raid fight an open-world boss inside the day after the account killed its summoned copy.
- Loot left behind: a raid that kills its boss and fails while it collects the loot leaves the loot on the corpse, which only the raid's leader can take. The boss pass sends that tab back with the bot's `loot` task (`iqBot.loot`), before anything else and up to three times while a try failed or left an item.
- What the simulator does not fight: a boss alone is all it knows, so a boss whose zone joins the fight is judged wrongly. An attack on Tunare brings the whole Plane of Growth into the fight, where its enemies heal her and other players cannot fight them meanwhile. Such a boss is named in `SIM_BLIND` (`tools/lib/boss-plan.mjs`) and gets no look, raid or credit raid, and its worth counts for no credit.
- Gear bosses: a gear target above level 50 that no group wins 5% of its fights against is watched too, and so is a casino boss whose kill is worth something to the gear plan (`gear` of `watchedBosses`, from the coordinator's target states and `bossValues`). The simulator fights it with the raid of all characters, a look goes to it when its respawn is due, and a raid kills it once a look sees it standing and the plan would pay the hour for its worth (`OPEN_BUY`). It stays watched until the gear plan no longer wants its drops, and Most wanted lists it "by raid" meanwhile.
- What the watch does not use yet: a raid on a summon reads no spawn list, so it does not see whether the same boss stands in the open world of its zone, and whether the list tells a private copy from the open-world boss is not known. Group 1 kills Lord Nagafen, Lady Vox, Faydedar and Phinigel Autropos alone in four simulated fights of four, which would need no raid time, and the next point says why the plan does not act on that.
- A group or the raid: a group of six can fight a boss in normal play, through a hunt that picks it in Enemy Targets (`directedKill` in `src/bot.js`), at no cost and with XP for all six. Group 1 killed Lady Vox at 51 and 52 that way (88,539 XP for the leader, against 435,600 for the leader alone in the raid) and Queen Velazul Di`zok, level 62, at 55. The boss plan still sends the raid, because nothing judges a group against a boss with its company. The simulator fights a boss alone and gave group 1 four kills of four in 79 s against Phinigel Autropos. In two fights group 1 chose at level 56 on 2026-11-12, he kept 58% of his 18,000 HP over 182 s beside one swirlspine seahorse, and beside two the leader stood at 549 of 3,591 HP after 96 s. No heal landed on him. The seahorses and he hit for 73 and 167 HP a second, cast area damage (Word Divine, Inferno of Al'Kabor) and dispelled the group's buffs and heals 16 times in the 96 s. Open: simulate a boss together with the spawns inside its aggro radius, for a group and for the raid, and check it against these fights.
- What the simulator lacks for that, from a first try on 2026-11-12: handed a second enemy's CombatInit two seconds into a fight, it gave the same fights event for event, so the helper never fought. And it is off against Phinigel Autropos alone. There group 1 kills him in 71 s, takes about 3,000 damage and sees one cast begin. Live, the group dealt him 5,192 damage in 182 s, a ninth of the simulated rate, while he began 20 casts, among them roots and dispels, and the party took 13,256 damage. What makes the difference is not established. The simulated boss barely casts, the simulated scene keeps every member in melee range for the whole fight, so a root costs nothing there, and the group fights in raid mode on orders, not as a party by its tactics. That gap has to close before a boss's company matters. Until then the live record decides for a group (`recordCounts`).
- The raid against Cazic-Thule, 2026-11-12: the simulator killed him in four fights of four, and the raid lost three. Three gaps were behind it. The live leader never fought while the bot kept 3D off. The boss's area spells hit nobody in the simulator, because its actors had no place. And the simulator's combat start left out the boss's weapon proc (`npcMeleeProc`, the spell list's `attack_proc` and `proc_chance`), Cazic-Thule's Panic, which procced 194 times in the third live fight and feared members, healers among them. In that fight, with the leader tanking, the boss dealt the raid 743 HP a second (268,372 melee and 143,114 from spells on 67 members in 554 s), the healers cast mostly Minor and Light Healing (1,152 of 1,613 heal casts), and the clerics' mana was gone by 230 s. With all three closed (model version 3), a simulated fight against him wipes with the boss at 21,230 HP, Innoruuk dies in 132 s with no deaths (live 125 s, none) and Lady Vox in 11 s. Every actor stands at the boss in the simulator, so his area spells reach more members than live, where casters stand at range. A boss the raid fought and lost to still waits twelve hours (`waiting` in the boss pass of `tools/iqd.mjs`), since the live fight decides first.
- Free summons: used every day on the killable bosses whose cooldown has passed, because an unused summon is lost.
- A summon of several bosses: the daily "Fear Golems (Dread, Fright & Terror)" names three NPCs of level 55 (`bossParts` in `tools/lib/boss-plan.mjs`). Its worth is what the three drop together, its kill chance the raid's simulated fights against each, with their times added (`tools/kill-odds.mjs`), and the raid task fights the one the summon names and then each of the others that still stands (`raidTask`, `also`). Not yet seen live: whether the three come at once, whether the raid's end leaves the others in reach, and what the daily list shows after the first kill. Read the first such raid's lines before trusting its worth.
- Order: the casino boss that stands goes first, since it blocks every other casino summon, and every other kill goes by the worth of its loot, the gear upgrades it brings, with the shorter raid first between equals (`nextBossKill`, `RAID_SETUP_MIN`). A free summon or a credit is the scarce currency, and the raid's minutes are not, since up to three raids run at once and most fights take seconds. No boss is planned for its own sake: a kill counts for the gear its drops bring.
- Casino credits and the potions left from before them: used in a raid by their holders. The server shows only the active character's credits, so the boss pass keeps each character's last read (db meta key `casinoCredits`), and the standing casino boss comes from the server's "resume" entry (`casinoStanding`).
- Several raids at once: a recruit is a snapshot of the character and leaves him to his group, and raid time is the account's. So inside paid time up to three raids run at once (`RAIDS_AT_ONCE` in `tools/iqd.mjs`), each in its own zone with its own free leader of 46 or more, the strongest group's leader for the best kill, and one casino raid at a time, since the account has one casino boss at a time. Outside paid time one raid starts, since two would each buy an hour. The open-world bosses that stand in a raid's zone come with it (`also`), so the four Skyshrine Crusaders take one trip and one setup.
- Raids on a summon or a potion run as soon as one is due, in the order above: a free summon comes back 24 hours after its use, so waiting for the second shifts the first. An hour of raid time costs 10,000 platinum, a third of what the account's sales bring in a day (30,241 platinum on 2026-11-11, when three hours were bought). A summon and a potion buy their hour. An open-world boss is killed inside an hour that runs, or buys one when the open-world kills of that hour are worth one hour of a group's progress together and the account's coin pays that hour and one more, which the next summon needs (`OPEN_BUY`, `canBuyHour`). That price is assumed: nothing measures what else the platinum would buy. The coin is the only limit on the hours, and the db meta key `raidHours` keeps them for the dashboard. A limit of two hours in 24 stood until 2026-11-13: three hours were bought the evening before, two of them for lost raids on Cazic-Thule, and for the next day no look went to Lord Nagafen and Talendor, due at 10:46 and 15:08 and worth 5.2 hours together, while the account held 123,000 platinum. The leader pays from its carried coin and pulls the account's largest balances until it holds the price: on 2026-11-12 the account had 49,000 platinum and no character over 9,300. A raid task runs for three times its simulated fight, between a quarter of an hour and 45 minutes, and longer while the boss still loses health and paid raid time lasts: Cazic-Thule's first raid ended at its 30 minutes about 75 s before the kill. When Run To Target leaves the leader out of reach, he walks to the boss with the ground navigation, since in that raid he stood 2,222 away and the boss killed 66 of 71 without its tank.
- Revives: the raid task brings its dead leader back with any revive, and a fallen member with a revive that costs no mana, the Cleric epic's, tanks before healers before the others (`revive` of `iqBot.raid`). A spell's revive costs a healer its mana and cast time, and nothing measures yet whether that pays, since the simulator orders no revives.
- A raid orders no slow and no debuff: in all four fights that ordered them the page stopped taking the server's messages at that second and the fight was lost (diary/2026-11-11-d.md).
- Zone sweeps: a raid in a zone whose standing enemies the gear plan wants kills every one of them it can reach, not only the boss it came for ([Zone sweeps](#zone-sweeps)).

### Zone sweeps

The gear plan wants drops of enemies above level 50 that no group kills and that stand on many spawn points: in Sleeper's Tomb the ancient sentries (26 points, 640 s respawn), the wyverns, drakeen and gargoyles (4 to 14 points, four hours), and in Veeshan's Peak Blood-Thirsty Racnor (32 points), Travenro the Skygazer (23), Qunard Ashenclaw and Milyex Vioren (11 each), all at 20 minutes. The simulated raid kills each of them in every fight. Before sweeps the watch treated such an enemy as one boss: the ancient sentry was raided six times in 32 hours, one kill a trip, 14 to 24 s each, while its other points stood.

A sweep keeps the raid in the zone after its target:

1. The coordinator gives an open-world raid a sweep list (`sweepNames` in `tools/lib/boss-plan.mjs`): every gear enemy of the watch in the zone that the simulated raid kills and whose kill is worth `SWEEP.minWorth` of a group-hour (`bossValues`), about what the raid minute of a kill and its walk cost at `OPEN_BUY`'s price.
2. After the named fights, the raid task fights the nearest enemy of the list that stands (`sweepZone` in `src/bot.js`), one after another, for up to `SWEEP.minutes` while paid raid time lasts. It keeps raid mode between fights, buys no hour for them, stops three minutes before paid time ends, and collects the loot once at the end.
3. A sweep stops at the first fight without a kill, and the task's result names the kills by enemy and why the sweep stopped ("swept 9 an ancient sentry, stopped as none of its enemies stands").
4. The hour's purchase counts every copy that stands: the sightings keep an enemy of several points by name with its living copies (`sightings`, the db meta key `bossesSeen`), and each copy adds its worth to the open-world kills that buy an hour (`OPEN_BUY`). The raid reads the zone's list at the sweep's end, as a look does.
5. Sweeps run inside paid time beside the other raids (`RAIDS_AT_ONCE`), each in its own zone. A gear enemy the raid killed is due again after its respawn time, at least half an hour, where a boss waits six hours.

To measure in the first sweeps: kills an hour, the walk between them, deaths, items per kill against the content's drop chances, and the worth of the drops against the hour's price. XP is no reason for a sweep: only the raid's leader gains it (224,000 to 242,000 a kill of level 56 enemies in Zharovane's raid of 2026-11-16, about 3.5 times a group kill), while the leader's five companions earn nothing in that time, so the group's XP stays about even and the hour costs platinum.
