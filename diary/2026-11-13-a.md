## How the audit went

Twelve read-only reviewers each read one slice of the code against the contract "report what changes behaviour, with the input that triggers it": the collector in two halves, the coordinator, the gear and epic planning, the bot in two halves, the bridge, the relay and supervisor, the dashboard, the documents, the simulator and reports, and the tests. They reported 150 findings. Eight owners, one per set of files, then checked each finding in the code and repaired it, and I read every diff. Three repairs I took back and made again: the guard's escape, the SDK's `attach` and the pull of an item by id. Beside the reviewers I read the day's log and the database, which showed the faults that cost play today.

Eleven commits (367 tests before, 430 after), the first seven each with the suite passing in a checkout of its own: `54bf767` simulator and reports, `c3a4a39` supervisor and relay, `9be33a9` epic chains, `10a53b5` coordinator, `bdf3e5f` collector, `c817c90` page code, `313ca58` dashboard, `1e4d420` documents, `0310db8` errands and raid loot, `9cea9a0` a busy tab's refusal, `2a7e96f` the raid bar's wording.

## What I had wrong first

- I took the repeated visits for one tab coming back inside the retry hour, through the rule that lets a further group hunt a target another group is on. Seven of 475 trips did. The visits come back at the first respawn the last one named, as designed, and the fault is in what a visit is expected to find.
- I wrote ten levels of wear as about 225 hours and the visits' items as worth 26. At level 55 a level takes 13 hours at the groups' pace, so it is about 130 hours and 15.
- A repair of the guard that came back from its owner wrote the escape's line after the zone change. The fight report measures an escape from that line to "escape done", and both would have fallen on one moment. I had read the diff before anything was deployed and put the line back where it was.
- Another returned repair made `attach` throw for any tab whose leader is not known during a canary, which is every fresh tab.
- I let every task that could not be queued hold its target for the retry time. The first pass after the deploy had a hunt refused by a tab paused for its upkeep, and a target's retry counts for every tab, so one busy tab would have kept a target from all groups for an hour. A busy tab's refusal now leaves no entry.

## Decisions to review

- A visit's chance to find its enemy counts the spawn tables as two visits (`ACQUIRE.upPrior`). A target looked for ten times without being found is then valued at a sixth of the tables' chance. If good targets drop out after a few unlucky looks, raise it.
- A failure that repeats at its step doubles its wait, up to a day. A step that fails for a reason that passes by itself, such as a server's quiet hour, then waits longer than it has to after the third time.
- A task that could not be queued waits its kind's retry time, two hours for an errand.
- An errand queued in a fight holds AutoLevel until the fight ends and the party has moved, up to five minutes.
- The lowest free group above an outgrown level band takes that band's piece hunts, within its hunt share of 15% of an hour. That group is the one furthest from 60.
- A page on its loading screen for three minutes is reloaded.
- A memory item needs a person only after 235 minutes, the longest the supervisor can take to give a tab its new process. Before that it was 50.
- A failed kill chance refresh raises an item that needs a person at once after two failures in a row.
- The documents lost their dated measurements and incident stories, by the repository's own rule: the "Where to hunt" table and the escape counts of the guide, the scout comparison and the uncertainty bands of the tools reference, the incident table and the failed-load timings of the fault handling plan, the old page inventory of the dashboard plan, and the table of attention kinds in the collector reference, which now points to `KINDS`. They are in the history of those files.
- The first nine commits are by component, each with several subjects in its body, where the rule asks for one subject each. The fixes of one file could not be told apart without staging hunk by hunk.
